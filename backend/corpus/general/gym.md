# Gym and Strength Training Guide

A practical guide to getting started with resistance training, structuring a program,
and training safely.

## Getting Started

### Before Your First Session

Most gyms recommend a brief equipment orientation for new members. Wear closed-toe shoes
with a flat, stable sole — running shoes with soft, cushioned heels are not ideal for
squats or deadlifts because they compress unevenly under load.

### Warming Up

A general warm-up of **5–10 minutes** of light cardio raises core temperature and joint
lubrication. This should be followed by **1–2 lighter sets** of the first exercise itself
("ramp-up sets") before working weight, rather than static stretching alone.

## Program Design

### Training Splits

A **full-body split** (3 sessions/week) suits beginners best, since each muscle group is
trained frequently enough to build technique quickly. A **push/pull/legs split** (6
sessions/week) suits more advanced lifters who can recover from higher per-session volume.

### Sets, Reps, and Load

For general strength, **3–5 sets of 4–8 reps** at a challenging load is a common range.
For muscular endurance or hypertrophy-focused work, **8–15 reps** at a lighter load is
more typical. Neither range is exclusively "correct" — both build muscle when taken close
to failure.

### Progressive Overload

Programs should increase difficulty over time — via added weight, added reps, added
sets, or improved technique — or adaptation stalls. A common approach is to add
**2.5–5 lb (1–2.5 kg)** per session on an exercise until a rep target can no longer be
hit with good form.

## Exercise Technique

### Squat

Feet roughly shoulder-width apart, knees tracking over the toes, and the hip crease
dropping **below the top of the knee** at the bottom of a full-depth rep. The chest stays
up throughout — a collapsing torso usually signals the weight is too heavy for current
mobility or bracing.

### Deadlift

The bar should stay close to the shins and thighs throughout the lift, with the hips and
shoulders rising **at the same rate** off the floor. A back that rounds under load is the
most common technical fault and the main driver of low-back strain in this lift.

### Bench Press

A stable **five-point setup** — head, upper back, and glutes on the bench, both feet
flat on the floor — provides a solid base. The bar path is not a straight vertical line;
it typically moves in a slight arc toward the lower chest and back up toward the
shoulders.

## Recovery

### Sleep

Most adults need **7–9 hours** of sleep per night for adequate muscle repair and hormone
regulation; consistently under 6 hours is associated with impaired strength recovery.

### Rest Between Sessions

Training the same muscle group with **at least 48 hours** between sessions is a common
guideline, though this varies with training volume, intensity, and individual recovery
capacity.

## Nutrition Around Training

### Protein

A commonly cited range for people strength training is **1.6–2.2 g of protein per kg of
body weight per day**, spread across multiple meals rather than consumed in one sitting.

### Pre- and Post-Workout

A meal containing carbohydrate and protein **1–3 hours before training** supports
performance. Post-workout, protein intake matters more for the day's total than the
exact minute it's consumed — the old "30-minute anabolic window" is now considered far
less strict than once believed.
