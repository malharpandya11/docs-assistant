# General Health and Wellness Guide

General reference information on sleep, nutrition, hydration, preventive care, and
common health metrics. This is educational material, not a substitute for advice from a
qualified medical professional.

## Sleep

### Recommended Duration

Adults generally need **7–9 hours** of sleep per night; teenagers need **8–10 hours**,
and school-age children **9–12 hours**. Consistently sleeping less than 6 hours is linked
to impaired immune function, mood, and cognitive performance.

### Sleep Hygiene

Keeping a **consistent sleep and wake time**, even on weekends, helps regulate the body's
circadian rhythm. Reducing screen exposure in the **hour before bed** is commonly
recommended, since blue light can suppress melatonin production.

## Nutrition Basics

### Macronutrients

The three macronutrients are **carbohydrates, protein, and fat**, each providing energy
(4, 4, and 9 kcal per gram respectively). No single macronutrient needs to be eliminated
for a generally healthy diet — balance and food quality matter more than avoidance.

### Fiber

Adults are commonly advised to get **25–38 grams of fiber per day** (roughly 25g for
women, 38g for men), from sources like vegetables, fruit, legumes, and whole grains.
Most adults in industrialized countries fall well short of this.

### Added Sugar

Health guidelines commonly suggest limiting added sugar to **under 10% of daily
calories**, with some organizations recommending under 5% for additional benefit. This
refers to added sugars, not naturally occurring sugars in fruit or dairy.

## Hydration

### Daily Intake

A commonly cited general guideline is **about 2–3 liters (8–12 cups) of total fluids per
day** for adults, though needs vary significantly with body size, activity level,
climate, and diet. Thirst is a reasonably reliable guide for most healthy adults.

### Signs of Dehydration

Dark yellow urine, headache, dizziness, and dry mouth are common early signs. Mild
dehydration (as little as **1–2% of body weight** in fluid loss) can measurably impair
concentration and physical performance.

## Preventive Care

### Routine Checkups

Adults are generally advised to have a general checkup **once a year**, including blood
pressure measurement, which should be checked **at least every 1–2 years** in adults with
normal readings, and more frequently if elevated.

### Common Screening Ages

Guidelines vary by country and risk factors, but commonly cited starting points include
colorectal cancer screening around **age 45**, and cardiovascular risk assessments
(cholesterol, blood pressure) starting in one's **20s**, repeated periodically.

## Common Health Metrics

### Blood Pressure

A reading below **120/80 mmHg** is generally considered normal. Readings consistently at
or above **130/80 mmHg** are commonly classified as high blood pressure (hypertension),
though exact thresholds vary slightly between guidelines.

### Resting Heart Rate

A typical resting heart rate for adults falls between **60–100 beats per minute**.
Well-conditioned athletes often have resting rates in the **40s–50s** due to a more
efficient cardiovascular system.

### BMI

Body Mass Index is calculated as weight in kilograms divided by height in meters
squared. A BMI of **18.5–24.9** is commonly classified as a "normal" range, though BMI
does not account for muscle mass, body composition, or distribution of fat, and is best
used as a rough population-level screening tool rather than an individual diagnosis.

## Mental Health Basics

### Stress and Recovery

Chronic, unmanaged stress is associated with elevated cortisol, disrupted sleep, and
increased cardiovascular risk over time. Regular physical activity, sufficient sleep, and
social connection are consistently associated with better stress resilience.

### When to Seek Help

Persistent low mood, loss of interest in activities, or anxiety that interferes with
daily functioning for **more than two weeks** is commonly used as a general guideline for
when to consult a mental health professional, rather than waiting for symptoms to
resolve on their own.
