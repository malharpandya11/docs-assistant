# Sports Fundamentals Guide

An overview of rules, scoring, and basic strategy for several widely played sports.

## Basketball

### Basic Rules

A standard game is played **5-on-5**, with **four quarters of 12 minutes** each in
professional play (FIBA uses 10-minute quarters). Teams must attempt a shot within the
**24-second shot clock**, or possession turns over.

### Scoring

A basket made inside the three-point line counts for **2 points**, outside it for
**3 points**, and a free throw counts for **1 point**. A "four-point play" happens when a
player is fouled while making a three-pointer and converts the resulting free throw.

### Common Violations

- **Traveling** — moving both feet without dribbling.
- **Double dribble** — dribbling, stopping, then dribbling again.
- **Goaltending** — blocking a shot on its downward path toward the basket.

## Soccer (Association Football)

### Basic Rules

A match consists of **two 45-minute halves**. Each team fields **11 players**, including
one goalkeeper. Offside is called when an attacking player is nearer the opponent's goal
line than both the ball and the second-last defender at the moment the ball is played to
them.

### Scoring and Set Pieces

A goal is scored when the entire ball crosses the goal line between the posts and under
the crossbar. A **penalty kick** is awarded for a foul inside the penalty area and is
taken from **11 meters (12 yards)** out.

### Cards

A **yellow card** is a caution; a second yellow in the same match results in an automatic
**red card** and ejection. A direct red card can also be shown for serious foul play,
denying an obvious goal-scoring opportunity, or violent conduct.

## Tennis

### Scoring a Game

Points are counted as **15, 30, 40, game**. A score of 40-40 is called "deuce," after
which a player must win **two consecutive points** to take the game — the first is
"advantage."

### Sets and Matches

A player must win **6 games** (by a margin of at least 2) to take a set. Most tournaments
use a **tiebreak at 6-6**, first to 7 points with a 2-point margin. Matches are typically
**best-of-3 sets**, or **best-of-5** in men's Grand Slam singles.

### Surfaces

The four Grand Slam tournaments are played on three different surfaces: hard court
(Australian Open, US Open), clay (French Open), and grass (Wimbledon) — each changing
ball bounce and player movement significantly.

## Swimming

### Competitive Strokes

The four strokes used in competition are **freestyle, backstroke, breaststroke, and
butterfly**. The **individual medley (IM)** requires a swimmer to complete all four in a
fixed order within one race.

### Pool Standards

An Olympic pool is **50 meters** long with **10 lanes** (8 used in competition). A
"short course" pool, common for training, is **25 meters**.

### False Starts

A single false start by any swimmer disqualifies that swimmer under current
international rules — there is no one-per-race allowance as in some older rule sets.
